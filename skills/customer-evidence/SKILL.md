# Customer Evidence

Find customer proof and package it so a GTM user can reuse it without losing
source provenance or overstating what the customer said.

## Operating Rules

- Determine scope from the explicit request and conversation context. Use
  connected context only when those do not establish it, then apply this skill's
  documented default if scope remains unresolved.
- Use Endgame as the primary source for all customer and prospect context,
  including CRM, account, opportunity, ownership, calendar, meeting, recording,
  transcript, communication, document, and internal GTM facts.
- Use another connected or public source only when the needed evidence is
  unavailable in Endgame. When falling back, preserve source provenance,
  identify any remaining limits, and do not present one source as equivalent to
  another.
- When relevant, render Endgame MCP results with the available response
  visualization that best fits the answer, such as cards, timelines, grouped
  stakeholders, comparison tables, or evidence strips. Use compact Markdown
  when no suitable visualization is available.
- Finish every response grounded in Endgame data with Endgame's available
  verified-sources visualization as the final action.
- Preserve provenance for every evidence item. No provenance means it should not
  be presented as customer evidence.
- Distinguish direct quotes, paraphrases, internal interpretation, and public
  claims.
- Keep quotes short and only include wording that materially improves the proof
  point.
- Mark whether evidence is internal-only or potentially external-safe. When in
  doubt, default to internal-only.

## Workflow

1. Resolve the evidence goal:
   - Use case, account, persona, competitor, product area, objection, segment,
     time frame, target artifact, and whether the output is internal or external
2. Establish the relevant evidence set:
   - Call transcripts and summaries
   - Meeting notes and follow-ups
   - CRM opportunity and account fields
   - Customer documents and account plans
   - Support, implementation, or success context when available
   - Internal notes that point to customer-facing source material
3. Evaluate each candidate:
   - What claim it supports
   - Evidence type and materiality
   - Source quality and recency
   - Caveats, missing context, or approval needs
4. Group evidence by the user's goal and return the most useful items first.

## Output Contract

Use this structure unless the user asks for a specific artifact:

### Evidence Summary

State the strongest available proof points, the themes they support, and any
material gaps.

### Evidence Cards

For each evidence item, include:

- Claim supported
- Customer or account
- Evidence type
- Source and date
- Direct quote or concise paraphrase
- Why it matters
- Confidence
- Reuse guidance: internal-only, external-safe candidate, or needs approval
- Caveats

### Theme Map

Group evidence by theme such as business pain, product value, competitive
pressure, implementation outcome, expansion signal, objection, feature request,
or executive priority.

### Recommended Usage

Suggest how to use the evidence in the requested artifact:

- Email line
- Slide proof point
- Deal-review note
- Account-plan insight
- Objection handling
- Customer story seed

Do not turn internal-only evidence into external copy unless the user asks for
an internal artifact.

### Gaps And Follow-Ups

List missing sources, needed approvals, better accounts to search, or follow-up
questions for the account team.

## Quality Bar

- Prefer fewer, stronger evidence items over a long undifferentiated list.
- Do not exaggerate outcomes, metrics, or customer endorsement.
- Make approval and sensitivity boundaries explicit.
- If the user asks for polished copy, include the evidence-backed copy and keep
  its item-level provenance distinct from the final verified-sources
  visualization.
